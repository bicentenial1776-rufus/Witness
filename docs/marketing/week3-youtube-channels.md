## Why founder pitches to YouTubers fail, and what works

1. Wrong size. One 200-creator campaign: ~25% replies from active channels under 20K subs, ~0.5% above 50K. The July silence is consistent with pitching big channels. Everyone in the top 5 here answers their own inbox.
2. Subject line answers "does this fit my channel?" Pattern: "[their series]: [what happens] — comp code inside." "Collaboration opportunity" gets archived.
3. Access in the first email, zero friction: comp code and a demo GEDCOM in the body. No "reply for a key."
4. Warm up two weeks first: substantive comments on their videos, then a short DM, then the email. Reported 3–5× response.
5. Ship a filmable asset: 60-second screen recording, b-roll without UI chrome, transparent logo PNG, and a demo tree seeded with a Filles du Roi line, a Grand-Pré family, and a Union regiment ancestor.
6. Pitch their own tree, not a review: "load your GEDCOM, film what it finds." A story, not an ad.
7. Timing: RootsTech is Mar 4–6 2027 and the call for presentations is open now; pitch "what's new" for Jan–Feb; DNA-gift season Dec–Jan for beginner channels. One follow-up after 5–7 days with "no worries if not a fit," then stop.

1. Ellen Thompson-Jennings — "Genealogy Step by Step: I gave an app my GEDCOM and it checked my Acadians against the 1755 deportation rolls."
2. Denyse Allen — reply to her "The Records Are Not the Reason You Don't Have a Story": "an app that writes one story a day from the record — does it pass the Chronicle Makers test?" Offer a story from her own Pennsylvania tree.
3. Larry Jones — follow his Stemma videos: "Before you trust ThruLines: 22 integrity checks on your own GEDCOM, on camera."
4. Lisa Elvin-Staltari — "FDR bonus episode: a Filles du Roi descendant tree matched live against colonial passenger lists."
5. Tech for Senior — "15-minute segment: genealogy on the iPad with no desktop software, free download, one-month trial."

Alternate: Projectkin "Project Recipe": "At the stone: camera, headstone, record, story in one walk."
