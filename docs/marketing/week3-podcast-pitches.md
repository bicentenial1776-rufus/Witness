## 5. The Family History AI Show — Mark Thompson & Steve Little
Route: makingfamilyhistory.com/contact
Slug: podcast-fhai

Subject: An expert-systems builder's answer to "what should the AI be allowed to say"

Mark and Steve,

Episode 42's turn toward desktop apps is the conversation I'd like to join.

I'm Rufus Howe, a long-time healthcare executive and builder of expert systems in
Red Hook, NY. I spent decades on software that had to reason over incomplete,
conflicting medical records and show its work to a clinician who would not accept
a verdict without the reasons. I built WitnessLives on the same rule for family
history.

The design choices your listeners argue about are the ones I had to make:

- The record is the authority. The daily story quotes what the tree holds; the
  AI's contribution, the world around the facts, is labeled plainly in the text.
- Nothing edits the tree. Every match against a register (colonial passenger
  lists, the Acadian rolls, Union regiments from Dyer) and every one of
  twenty-two integrity checks arrives as a question with its reasons written out,
  graded documented or probable, for the reader to judge.
- Vision reads headstones with an honesty rule: bracketed gaps where the stone is
  illegible, never a guess. The reading is then scored against the tree with the
  scoring shown.
- Cost discipline: story generation runs on a mid-tier model; the daily reader
  costs a few dollars a month in AI, which is why it's $19.99 a year.

I'd be glad to demo any of that on one of your own trees with complimentary
accounts, and to talk about where the approach fails, which it does.

Thank you,
