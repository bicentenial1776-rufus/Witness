Seven walks through Witness, and where they break

The Issue shipped, the app hangs together — so this pass stopped reading the map and walked the roads. Seven realistic journeys across iPhone, iPad, and web, each pushed until something gave; a register of every gap found; and an answer to the Nearby-tab question. As of 8 August, every gap in the register is closed.

01The Nearby question, answered first

Yes — fold Nearby into Map. The code has already voted. Nearby's own header comment reads "Distance is this tab's only dimension (eras live on the Map tab)" — it knows it's half a feature — and it already contains an internal list | map toggle, meaning the app currently ships two maps. The tabs divide one question ("where?") by which coordinate is fixed: Map fixes the era and roams space; Nearby fixes your position and roams distance. No user thinks in those terms.

Merged shape: Map gains a NEAR ME mode — tap it, the map flies to you, the radius chips appear, pins become your-people-within-N-miles, and the era segmented control keeps working (which fixes a real omission: Nearby currently has a century filter but no era language, so the two screens even disagree about time). The graveyard moment survives intact: open app → Map → Near me is the same tap count as open app → Nearby.

And leave the freed slot empty. Four tabs — Home · Tree · Explore · Map — is not a poverty; it's the four verbs (read · work · ask · go). Filling the slot "because it's free" is exactly the opportunism this whole exercise is correcting. The strongest future claimant is Street View when it becomes real; parking the slot keeps the seat warm for it. (This reverses the July 25 IA decision that gave Nearby its own tab — that decision predates the Issue, and the field moment it protected is better served by the planned "I'm Here" lock-screen notification than by a resident tab.)

02The walks

W1

The Sunday twenty minutes

iPhone

Regular reader, tree lives on Ancestry, nothing new imported. "What's here that I didn't know last week?"

Home / NO. 32→ lead→ Portrait→ sibling→ birthplace→ place→ another Portrait→ …back ×5 to Home to find piece two

Breaks

The wander is exactly right until the reader wants the next piece — the only road back to the issue is the back button, five screens deep. The brief promised "next in this issue" as the Portrait's return path; it isn't built. Gap G1.

W2

The curiosity thread

iPhone · web

Same reader, taps the week's Tree Check piece: "Josiah Haskell's daughter was born before his marriage."

Home→ curiosity prompt→ Josiah's Portrait (finding shown in place)→ Open the Tree Check→ the workbench re-downloads what Home already fetched

Half fixed

The prompt now lands on the person, not the 443-row workbench — fixed this pass. But tree-health.tsx still fetches its own audit data instead of sharing the curiosities cache, so the workbench hop re-runs ~30 paginated requests Home already made. Gap G2.

W3

The full Ancestry round trip

iPhone → Ancestry app → weeks later

The Archives desk says two draft cards await judgment. The reader confirms one and wants it in their real tree.

Home→ Archives→ card → person ✓→ This is them→ Add to Ancestry (URL copied, app opens)→ paste as web link→ …weeks pass… re-import = a NEW tree

Breaks at the far end

The outbound leg is genuinely good — clipboard + universal link is the honest bridge around Ancestry's missing write API. The return leg has a structural crack: import is not idempotent, so the re-import that "brings the link home" creates a fresh tree, and everything keyed by tree_id — marks, NARA decisions, the new findings ledger, back issues — stays behind on the old one. Rulings alone survive (xref-keyed). The pulse module already solved carry-forward for Tree Health verdicts; the same treatment is owed to the rest. Gap G3 — the deepest one in the app.

W4

The graveyard

iPhone, in the field

Standing in a cemetery in Worcester on a Saturday. Which of ours are here?

Nearby→ radius 5mi→ person→ Portrait → household → siblings→ phone in pocket, tab spent the rest of the year idle

Holds — but overpays

The flow works and the Portrait's new doors make the graveside reading much richer. The break is economic, not functional: a permanent tab for a some-Saturdays feature. Fold into Map (§01). The real field entry the brief promised — the "I'm Here" lock-screen nudge when you pass through ancestral territory — remains unbuilt and would serve this moment better than any tab. Gap G4.

W5

The first hour

iPhone, fresh install

New beta user, GEDCOM exported per the guide, first import finishing.

onboarding→ import→ recovery code→ home person→ Home / NO. 32→ lead has no note · Archives desk empty for days · map pins trickle in

Thin, and doesn't say why

The lead works immediately (digest computes from events) and the pattern desk works (place parsing needs no geocoding). But the hero's record-grounded note is written by a daily job, NARA matching waits on a pg_cron worker, and geocoding fills the map over hours. First-hour reader sees a half-empty issue with no explanation. The thin-week principle says don't fabricate — right — but candor is owed: a one-line "first issue; the presses are still warming up — richer next week" would convert confusion into anticipation. Gap G5.

W6

The shared story

web, signed out → member

A cousin receives a story link in the family group chat.

unfurl card→ /shared/[token]→ story renders→ sign-in→ lands on THEIR home — the shared person is gone

Breaks at the handoff

Reading works; the bridge into membership drops its cargo. A cousin moved to sign up by a particular ancestor arrives at a generic Home (or an empty import screen — the cousin has no GEDCOM; the tree is the family's, which is really the V3 cousin-network problem showing its face early). At minimum the shared token should survive sign-in and land the new member back on that story. Gap G6.

W7

The iPad evening

iPad, native app

The armchair session — the form factor the brief calls the reading device. Also: Greg's platform.

Home→ phone layout, stretched to 10 inches

Breaks on arrival

useBroadsheet() is Platform.OS === 'web' && width ≥ 900 — the broadsheet carrier, the app's best reading layout, is unreachable on the device most suited to reading. An iPad in Safari at app.witnesslives.com gets a better Witness than the installed app. The gate is one line; the audit of broadsheet components for native rendering is the real (modest) work. Gap G7.

04Priority, if it were mine to order

  • Scheduled: G7 (native iPad broadsheet) before Street View.
  • Explicitly not now: filling the fifth tab slot; the cousin network (G6 gets the token-through-auth patch only).
