The Screens

Explore.

The questions your tree can answer, in reading order: the Library’s full catalog, the Ways in, and the shelf of moments scored for your family this month — now ending with a place to keep your own questions. Search stays at the top as the fast path.

How you get here: the Explore tab.

Awaiting screenshot: the Explore tab in its new order — the search box, THE LIBRARY card, and the first WAYS IN card in frame. Target: shots/explore.png (replaces the pre-reorder shot).
  1. One search box, three kinds of answers. A name (“Elizabeth Dane”) finds people; a place (“Worcester”) finds everyone with an event recorded there, each tagged with the place that surfaced them; anything else matches historical moments in the library. People results say how many the search found — every match, counted — and arrive newest first, twenty-five to a page, with ‹ Newer and Older › beneath the list to walk back through the generations.
  2. The Library — the full catalog of questions, century by century, every row already counted against your own tree.
  3. Ways in — three doors: Where your family lived, Patterns in your family (where it began, the moves it made, the oceans it crossed, the couples who turned out to be kin), and Your whole ancestry, read at once — every recorded ancestor woven into one essay.
  4. From your family’s history — the shelf: a few events chosen for this tree this month, sometimes wearing an anniversary label, each with its live count: “1,393 of your ancestors were alive ›”.
  5. Your own questions — type a question beneath the shelf and it runs through the ordinary search and is kept, on this device only, for the next visit: “Your questions — tap one to ask it again.” The × forgets one. A question the tree can’t answer yet simply waits for the tree to grow.
Awaiting screenshot: a people search mid-page — a common name (“Henry”) with the count line (“47 people — newest first, showing 26–50”) and the ‹ Newer · Page 2 of 2 · Older › controls under the list. Target: shots/explore-search.png
  1. The count line — every match the tree holds, not just the visible page; nothing is silently cut off.
  2. Newer / Older — the pages are ordered by birth year, so paging forward is walking back in time; undated people close the final pages.

The National Archives lives on the Tree tab, where the deciding work is; Explore stays the wandering tab.

Reading a result screen

Tapping any event opens the same result anatomy, wherever you came from:

Awaiting screenshot: a result screen (any event) — the headline count with its new “Documented and probable together” line, the Related / Everyone chips, and a dashed probable card in frame. Target: shots/query-results.png (replaces the shot with the old count line).
  1. The headline count — how many were alive during the event, documented and probable together: the fuller picture leads. The line beneath says how the number is built — how many carry recorded years, how many rest on a typical lifespan (those cards wear a dashed border).
  2. Related / Everyone — Related filters to your featuring scope; Everyone shows the whole tree. Nothing is ever hidden — Related is a lens, not a wall.
  3. Each person’s card — name with its lineage mark, your relationship, their parentage, their years, and their age when the event began. Tap through to their page.
  4. Share this discovery — renders the finding as a card image you can send to family.
Arriving from a “lived through” tag on someone’s page pins that person to the top of the results, flagged THE ANCESTOR YOU CAME FROM — the count and tabs stay honest; the pin only reorders.
In the field. With no signal — a cemetery, a back road — the search answers from a copy of the tree saved on the phone, under the line “From your saved copy — searched without a connection.” Names and places still match; the copy refreshes itself whenever the app runs with a connection.
