The Screens

Explore.

The questions your tree can answer: search over people, places, and history; a shelf of historical moments scored for your family this month; and the doorways into every geographic and structural query.

How you get here: the Explore tab.

1 2
  1. One search box, three kinds of answers. A name (“Elizabeth Dane”) finds people; a place (“Worcester”) finds everyone with an event recorded there, each tagged with the place that surfaced them; anything else matches historical moments in the library.
  2. From your family’s history — the shelf: a few events chosen for this tree this month, sometimes wearing an anniversary label. The line that matters is the live count: “1,393 of your ancestors were alive ›”.

Below the shelf sit The Library — the full catalog of questions, century by century, every row already counted against your own tree — and two Ways in: Where your family lived (every state, province, and country in the tree) and Patterns in your family, one door to four findings — where the family began, the moves it made, the oceans it crossed, and the couples who turned out to be kin. The National Archives moved to the Tree tab, where the deciding work lives.

Reading a result screen

Tapping any event opens the same result anatomy, wherever you came from:

1 2 3 4
  1. The headline count — how many were alive during the event — and beneath it, how many are documented (dates prove it) versus probable (inferred; those cards wear a dashed border).
  2. Related / Everyone — Related filters to your featuring scope; Everyone shows the whole tree. Nothing is ever hidden — Related is a lens, not a wall.
  3. Each person’s card — name with its lineage mark, your relationship, their parentage, their years, and their age when the event began. Tap through to their page.
  4. Share this discovery — renders the finding as a card image you can send to family.
Arriving from a “lived through” tag on someone’s page pins that person to the top of the results, flagged THE ANCESTOR YOU CAME FROM — the count and tabs stay honest; the pin only reorders.
