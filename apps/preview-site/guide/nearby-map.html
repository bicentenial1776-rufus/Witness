The Screens

The Map & Near me.

One tab, two grips on the same question — where? The Map shows the whole world of the tree, era by era. Near me fixes your own position instead and asks who in your family has history around where you’re standing, with distance as its only dimension.

How you get here: the Map tab. The Near me chip beside the era control swaps the whole view; a Places chip brings the map back. Near me asks for location permission the first time — it reads your position when you open it; nothing tracks you in the background.

Near me

Awaiting screenshot: the Map tab in Near me mode — headline count, radius slider, the single scrolling filter row (Your family · Everyone │ eras │ events), and the first place sections in frame. Target: shots/near-me.png.
  1. The headline — how many of your family’s people have recorded history within the current radius of you.
  2. The radius slider — 1 to 100 miles, with half the track devoted to 1–10: the cemetery-and-town range where precision matters. The chip on the left flips between List and Map views of the same results; the Places chip in the corner returns to the full Map.
  3. Your family · Everyone — who counts. By default the list holds your own people, honoring the same direct-line-or-all-blood choice as “Who gets featured” on the You tab; Everyone widens to the whole tree — in-laws, unconnected records, all of it. When none of your relatives are in range but others are, the headline says exactly that rather than reading as empty ground.
  4. One filter row — the kinship pair, the era chips (All eras · 1600s · 1700s…), and the event chips (Birth · Death · Burial…, multi-select — standing in a cemetery you want Burial and Death together) share a single row that scrolls sideways, thin rules between the groups. Everything is still one tap; the stack no longer eats the screen.
  5. Place sections — each headed by the place name and its distance from you (“North Burial Ground · 0.4 mi”), one card per person: name, your relationship, the events with years, and a Find A Grave › link where the record carries one.

Every person card opens their page; in Near me’s map view, tapping a pin’s callout opens the place itself — everyone recorded there, however far from you. The pins honor the same filters as the list: a marker never claims people the list would not show.

The Map

Awaiting screenshot: the Map tab in Places mode — pins, era control, and the Near me and Sat chips in frame. Target: shots/map.png (replaces the pre-merge shot).
  1. The pins — every place in your tree with recorded activity, up to the 300 busiest for the chosen era.
  2. The era control — All · 1600s · 1700s · 1800s · 1900s: the map reframes itself to where the family actually was in that century.
  3. Near me and Sat — Near me swaps to the view above; Sat flips the muted cartography to satellite imagery with labels.
  4. The mapping progress banner — how many of the tree’s places have been located so far. It appears while geocoding is still working and retires when the job is done.
After an import, placing your places on the map takes time — geocoding runs on the server for a while, and the map picks up newly-placed pins each time you return. An emptier-than-expected map right after import is filling, not finished.
