The Screens

Nearby & the Map.

Two geographic views of the same record. Nearby asks one question — who in my family has history near where I’m standing? — with distance as its only dimension. Map shows the whole world of the tree, era by era.

How you get here: the Nearby and Map tabs. Nearby asks for location permission the first time — it reads your position when you open it; nothing tracks you in the background.

Nearby

Screenshot pending — the Nearby tab list view with results within a few miles
(save as guide/shots/nearby.png)
  1. The headline — how many of your family’s people have recorded history within the current radius of you.
  2. The radius slider — 1 to 100 miles, with half the track devoted to 1–10: the cemetery-and-town range where precision matters. The chip on the left flips between List and Map views of the same results.
  3. Era chips — when the places found span centuries, chips (All eras · 1600s · 1700s…) filter to people with events in that century.
  4. Place sections — each headed by the place name and its distance from you (“North Burial Ground · 0.4 mi”), listing everyone with an event recorded there: name, your relationship, and the events with years.

Every person card opens their page; in the map view of Nearby, tapping a pin’s callout opens the place itself — everyone recorded there, however far from you.

The Map

Screenshot pending — the Map tab with pins across the tree’s world
(save as guide/shots/map.png)
  1. The pins — every place in your tree with recorded activity, up to the 300 busiest for the chosen era.
  2. The era control — All · 1600s · 1700s · 1800s · 1900s: the map reframes itself to where the family actually was in that century.
  3. Sat — flips the muted cartography to satellite imagery with labels.
After an import, placing your places on the map takes time — geocoding runs on the server for a while, and the map picks up newly-placed pins each time you return. An emptier-than-expected map right after import is filling, not finished.
