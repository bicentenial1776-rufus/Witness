Concepts

The ideas behind the screens.

Six ideas repeat across every screen in Witness. Read them once here and the marks and lines everywhere else explain themselves.

1 · The home person

Everything in Witness is seen from one vantage point: the home person — you, designated on the You tab (“You are Rufus Scott Howe”). Every relationship label (“your 7th great-grandmother”), every mark, and every “Related” filter is computed from that person outward. Change the home person and the whole tree re-orients.

2 · Direct, blood, distant, and no relation

Everyone in the tree carries two facts, seen from the home person: which of four categories they fall in, and the exact relationship in words. The category is what you read beside a name — Direct line ▾, Blood ▾, Distant ▾ — and the marks show the same thing at a glance. Tap the category and the exact words unfold beneath it: Blood · your 3rd cousin twice removed ▴. One calculation, two altitudes.

Direct — your ancestors and your descendants: the people you descend from, and who descend from you. Your 7th great-grandmother. Your grandson.

Blood — everyone who shares an ancestor with you without being on the line itself: cousins at any distance, great-aunts and great-uncles, nieces and nephews. Your 3rd cousin twice removed. Your half-brother.

Distant — the people who married in, and the family they brought with them: in-laws, step-family, and the spouses of your ancestors who are not themselves your ancestors. Your father-in-law. Your stepmother. The wife of your great-grandfather. A person is distant when exactly one marriage crosses the path between you; two marriages is not a relationship, it is a coincidence of the file.

No relation — no path at all: unattached branches, research trees merged in whole, a spouse’s spouse’s people. These carry no label, because there is nothing true to say. The blank is the answer.

Adoption keeps the family it belongs to. An adopted child is on the line, and the words say how — adoptive father, adopted son, foster mother — and where a birth parent is recorded alongside an adoptive one, both are named. Step-relations are distant, which is a statement about blood and never about belonging.

3 · The lineage marks

Wherever people are listed, a small mark next to a name places them at a glance:

⇅ Direct line. An up-and-down arrow — this person is on your vertical line, ancestor or descendant.

💧 Blood. A drop — blood, but off the line: a cousin, a great-aunt, a collateral branch.

🔗 Distant. A link — connected by marriage: an in-law, a step-parent, the second wife of a great-grandfather.

No mark. No relation to you that the tree can trace. The absence is deliberate; an unmarked name is itself information.

4 · “Who gets featured” — celebration, not visibility

The You tab has a setting with three choices: Direct line, Blood relatives, or Blood and married-in. It answers exactly one question: who counts as family in the digest, the Sunday reminder, and “Related” filters — who gets celebrated. Each choice contains the one before it.

It never hides anyone. Every query, the Family Graph, Tree Health, and every list still show the whole tree; an “Everyone” toggle on result screens makes that explicit. Everyone keeps their relationship label — they just aren’t featured. The one place the choice also sets a default filter is Near me, which opens on your own people — standing in a cemetery, the question is almost always “which of these are mine?” — with Everyone one tap away there too.

5 · Parentage lines

Under names throughout the app runs a quiet line of descent: “Daughter of William Towne and Joanna Blessing.” It names the person’s recorded parents — both when the record has both, one otherwise. A person with no parentage line simply has no parents recorded in the tree: the oldest generation, and most people who married in.

6 · Documented and probable

When Witness answers a question like “who was alive during this event,” each match is either documented (solid card border — the dates prove it) or probable (dashed border, labeled “probable” — inferred from partial dates). The headline count tells you how many of each. And one privacy rule holds everywhere: a person recorded as living keeps their story private — no AI biography, no featuring of their details.
