The Screens

Home — the daily reading.

Home is a daily paper. Each midnight a new issue is set — numbered by the day of the year, the same one on every device you own — and it prints exactly five pieces, in the same order every day. Every pick is made from the date itself, so nothing here is random and nothing demands action: it’s a paper to read with coffee, and tomorrow brings another.

How you get here: the Home tab — the app opens here. The gear in the top corner opens You (account, trees, and preferences).

Awaiting screenshot: the Home tab as today’s issue — the NO. dateline, ON THIS DAY, and the ANCESTOR OF THE DAY card in frame. Target: shots/home-issue.png.
  1. The dateline — NO. 239 · THURSDAY, AUGUST 27. The issue number is the day of the year; a new issue is set at midnight.
  2. ON THIS DAY — a family anniversary falling today: a birth or death, how many years ago, and where. On a day with no dated anniversary, a historical moment your family lived through stands in, with how many of your people were alive for it. THIS WEEK IN YOUR FAMILY › opens This Week.
  3. ANCESTOR OF THE DAY — one life from your featured people, chosen for the date, with years, how they connect to you, and their note when the daily writer has one. THEIR FULL STORY › opens their person page.
  4. A GENERATIONAL STORY — today’s line: one recorded descent, founder to reader, told as a story. READ THE LINE › opens it in full.
  5. FAMILY GRAPH OF THE DAY — one household, chosen for the date from the ones whose record can really be drawn (a dated marriage, a real sibship). OPEN THE GRAPH › draws it on the Stage.
  6. THE NATIONAL ARCHIVES — how many federal records await your judgment. LOOK THROUGH 10 › opens ten of them right here — each a candidate you confirm or set aside without leaving the page — and the footnote opens the full Archives.

Once you tap into a piece, the issue follows you: the person page carries a slim band — NO. 239 · NEXT: THE GENERATIONAL STORY › — so the next piece is one tap, however deep the reading has wandered. Dismiss it with the × and it stays quiet for the sitting.

A brand-new tree’s first issues run thin, and the front page says so — the notes are written by a daily job, archive matches by a scheduled worker, map pins by the geocoder. Richer issues arrive as the presses warm over the first days.
