The Screens

Family Seats.

One person did the research. Family seats hand the finished thing to the people it’s about: up to four family members read your tree with you — the stories, the map, the confirmed records — each from their own place in the family. They can look; only you can change.

How you get here: the Family card on the You tab. Invited family arrive through the link you send them; they never need this page to find their way in.

Inviting someone

Screenshot needed: the You tab’s Family card with at least one member seated and one invitation waiting — stage it with the seat-test account so both rows show. Save as shots/you-family.png.
  1. The roster — everyone seated in this tree, by the name they joined under, with the date they started reading. Remove ends a seat on the spot; nothing they imported themselves is touched.
  2. Invitation waiting — a link you’ve sent that hasn’t been used. Each works once and expires after seven days; Take back cancels it early.
  3. Invite family › — makes a fresh link and opens the share sheet. Send it however you talk: Messages, mail, anything. When all four seats are taken, this becomes a plain sentence saying so.
A seat is access, not a copy. Remove someone (or let the plan lapse) and the tree simply disappears from their app — there is nothing to claw back, because nothing ever left your account.

What they see

Screenshot needed: the invitation landing page — “Rufus has kept a seat for you” — as the invited person sees it before signing in (open an invite link in a signed-out browser). Save as shots/join-landing.png.
  1. Who is inviting, into what — your first name and the tree’s name and size, shown before any account exists. Creating one takes an email and a password; the seat carries its own access, so they are never asked to pay.
  2. Take your seat — one tap, then the only onboarding question a companion gets: who are you? They find themselves in the tree, and from that moment every relationship in the app is told from where they sit — your “3rd great-grandfather” may be their “4th”, and the app knows.

A companion’s Witness is a reading edition. Home prints their weekly issue, the Portrait tells each life with the stories and the confirmed records — the verified graves, the attached stones and their photographs — the Map and Near me answer from wherever they stand, and the Family Graph plays its households. They can share an ancestor’s card onward, attributed to them. What they don’t see is the workbench: Tree Health, the Archives review, research briefs, margin corrections and notes are yours alone, and for them those doors simply aren’t on the wall — nothing greyed out, nothing locked-looking.

Living people are visible inside the family — a seat is family, not the public. The public sharing rule is unchanged: nothing shareable beyond the family ever includes a living person.

Their own trees

A seat is a full account. If your sister imports her own GEDCOM, it sits beside your shared tree in her list, hers to work as owner — and your tree shows “shared with you” with Leave this tree where Delete would be. Leaving works the same as being removed: access ends, her own data stays hers.
