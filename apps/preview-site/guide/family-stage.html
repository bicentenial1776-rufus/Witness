The Screens

The Family Graph.

One household — a marriage and its children — drawn as vertical threads against falling time. Each thread is a life; the amber enclosure is the marriage; the reading line lies across them and boxes every age in the household in a single year. And the graph is a road, not a picture: tap any ribbon to open that person, and follow the ↑ and ↓ doors up and down the generations.

How you get here: Tree tab → the Family Graph door (or the Register, the table of contents of all households); or from a person’s page, Family Graph › under the “House of” line — offered only when the household can actually be drawn. How you leave: “← BACK” returns to whatever screen you came from — a person’s page, the Register, or the previous graph in a chain of generation hops.

Awaiting screenshot — shots/family-stage.png: the Family Graph on a multi-child household, family details folded, with ↑ badges on both parents, a lineage mark beside at least one name, and the reading line mid-family.
  1. “← BACK” — one tap to wherever you came from.
  2. FAMILY DETAILS ▾ — the household’s dates, how long the family lasted, and the color legend fold away here so the graph keeps its room; tap to open them.
  3. The threads — each life as a vertical ribbon from birth downward, carrying the person’s full name. A “?” caps a ribbon whose end is unrecorded — Witness never invents a lifespan. Tap any ribbon to open that person’s page; back returns you here.
  4. The ↑ and ↓ doors — an ↑ badge on a parent leads to the household they grew up in; a ↓ badge on a child leads to the family they went on to raise. No badge means that road isn’t in the record yet. Each ribbon keeps three lanes: doors and lineage marks hug its left edge, the age box rides the right edge, and the name owns the middle — nothing covers the name at any year.
  5. The lineage marks — the amber ⇅ rides beside a direct ancestor’s name; the drop marks blood kin off the line; the link marks someone who married in. No mark means no traceable relation — absence is the marker.
  6. The amber enclosure — the marriage: it opens at the wedding and closes when the first spouse dies. A spouse’s thread running past it is widowhood.
  7. The reading line and band — the fixed amber line; the years roll beneath it, the small boxes are ages in that year, and the band below puts the year in words. Drag the graph for fine time, the slider for coarse.
  8. SWEEP THE YEARS — plays time forward through the family’s whole span; tap again to hold a year.
What it takes to draw a graph. A household appears here once the record can date it: a dated marriage, a birth-dated parent, and at least one child with a recorded birth year. A household thinner than that can’t be drawn — the screen says so plainly rather than showing you somebody else’s family. The remedy is in your hands: add those dates to your tree at its source and refresh, and more households — and more ↑ ↓ doors — appear.
If the head of the household married more than once, labeled MARRIAGES chips above the graph switch between them — each spouse named in full, each set re-scaling the years to its own span. A spouse who outlived the union is drawn to the head’s next marriage, not to their death: the record has no divorce dates, so the next marriage is the honest cutoff.
