The Screens

The Family Stage.

One household — a marriage and its children — drawn as vertical threads against falling time. Each thread is a life; the amber enclosure is the marriage; the reading line lies across them and boxes every age in the household in a single year.

How you get here: Tree tab → the Register (the table of contents of households) → tap a family; or from a person’s register, See this household as a length of time › — offered only when the household can actually be drawn. How you leave: “← FAMILIES” returns to the Register in one tap, however deep you’ve wandered.

1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
  1. “← FAMILIES” — back to the Register, always one tap.
  2. The household’s name and years, and how long the family lasted — from the marriage to the death of its last surviving child.
  3. The legend — thread colors: man, woman, a pale outlined ribbon for a child who died before 18, faded for a person whose death went unrecorded.
  4. The threads — each life as a vertical ribbon from birth downward. A “?” caps a ribbon whose end is unrecorded — Witness never invents a lifespan.
  5. The amber enclosure — the marriage: it opens at the wedding and closes when the first spouse dies. A spouse’s thread running past it is widowhood.
  6. The reading line — the fixed amber line; the years roll beneath it, and the small boxes on each thread are ages in that year. Drag the stage to move time finely; the slider below is the coarse control.
  7. The reading band — the line’s year in words: how long married, who’s at home under eighteen, how many of the group are living, and children’s marriages as the line passes them.
  8. SWEEP THE YEARS — plays time forward through the family’s whole span; tap again to hold a year.
If the head of the household married more than once, chips above the stage switch between marriages — each set re-scales the years to its own span. A spouse who outlived the union is drawn to the head’s next marriage, not to their death: the record has no divorce dates, so the next marriage is the honest cutoff.
The Stage needs a dated marriage and at least one child with a recorded birth year. A household whose record is thinner than that can’t be drawn — the screen says so plainly rather than showing you somebody else’s family.

The family group sheet

The FAMILY GROUP chip lifts a sheet listing every member of the household — name, years, role, lineage mark, and parentage. Tap a person to open their full page; the back arrow returns here. BRIEFS lists any research briefs open on this household’s members.

1 2 3
  1. Each member’s line — name with lineage mark, years, and their role in this household (HEAD, SPOUSE, CHILD).
  2. The parentage line — for children here it will always be present; for the head and spouse only if their own parents are in the tree.
  3. “THEIR STAGE ›” — a child who married has a stage of their own; this follows the family onward a generation.
