The Screens

A person’s page.

The hub of the whole app — every list, card, and pin eventually lands here. One page holds who they were, how they connect to you, what the world was doing around them, and exactly how the record knows it.

How you get here: from anywhere — a piece of this week’s issue, a digest card, a query result, the family group sheet, a Near me listing, a search hit, or another person’s register.

  1. The issue band — NO. 239 · NEXT: THE GENERATIONAL STORY › — appears while you’re reading today’s issue: the next piece is one tap, however deep the visit has wandered. The × rests it for the sitting.
  2. The identity block — the name with its lineage mark; the small ⇅ beside it turns the perspective around (below); a documented ocean crossing earns a small flag — Atlantic crossing — that opens every crossing in the tree. Beneath: the record line (woman/man · years · birthplace), then the parentage line. The birthplace is a doorway — it opens the place itself — and when the town is on the map, a ▾ beside it unfolds a small map with the town pinned, right in place. On a direct ancestor the line ends with the compass: gen 7 · father’s side. A headstone already read at the stone shows its photograph here too.
  3. “Direct line ▾” — the relationship lede, category first. Tap it and the exact words unfold — Direct line · your 2nd great-grandmother ▴ — with See the line ▾ opening the person-by-person chain right on the page: each generation with its years and parentage, everyone tappable. A person in your file with no path at all says so plainly: No relation.
  4. The four tabs — OVERVIEW · LIFE & TIMES · FAMILY · SOURCES. Overview is the at-a-glance reading: a sentence built from the record itself (born where, which child of whom, married whom and when), the Parents and Spouse lines, the events this life overlapped as a row of pills, and Family at a glance with its sibling and children counts. The links at the bottom of each block jump straight to the deeper tab.
  5. LIFE & TIMES — Their story, one continuous read that begins writing itself the moment the tab opens. The AI-written biography comes first (generated once, then kept, with its plain disclosure — the record, not the story, is the authority); the history around this life, drawn from sources, follows in the same panel; the story export and YOUR NOTE close it. Then Alive during… in full: each event unfolds to a line anchored by this person’s own age when it began, with Everyone who lived through this › beneath. Then Lived near — contemporaries in your tree with a documented event within 25 miles, nearest first, each with their relation to you — and The record, the dated events on one thread.
  6. FAMILY — the family chart (two generation bands, the amber tie the marriage, an amber border meaning that person has a story of their own), then the running head — House of Stephen Gates & Anne Neave · fourth of 13 children — and the register: Parents, Brothers & sisters with this person highlighted (the ~0 and ~2 beside short lives are ages at death), then each marriage with its children, with Family Graph › to see the household on the Stage.
  7. SOURCES — the research desk. How the record knows this person: the citations grouped by source, with the facts each supports and excerpts where the record has them; the burial-record walk to Find A Grave; your margin corrections; the Tree Check’s prompts naming this person; candidate records from the National Archives; and the row of ways out — Research › (a brief), the tree’s own provider (Ancestry or FamilySearch), and Share ›.

The perspective lens. The small ⇅ beside a name re-anchors the whole relationship framing on that ancestor for the sitting: every Portrait then reads SEEN FROM ABIGAIL MAXEY in a slim band, labels are recomputed from her seat in the tree — her husband, her granddaughter — and the line traces to her instead of to you. RESET ✕ hands the tree back. Nothing is changed underneath: who you are in the tree stays yours.

A person recorded as living gets a quieter page: “… appears to be living, so Witness keeps their story private.” No biography, no world context, no share link — the record and the register remain.
In the field. With no signal, the page renders from the phone’s saved copy of the tree — identity, dates, birthplace, and the whole family register — under the line “From your saved copy”. The live sections stand down quietly and return with the signal.

Burial record — for anyone not living, the Sources tab can walk you to Find A Grave: Find burial record › opens a search already filled with what the record knows, you browse and judge in your own browser, and when you’ve found the right memorial you paste its link back to confirm it. Witness stores only the URL you personally verified — imported citations claim a memorial; only you can confirm one.

The page keeps your own hand close. YOUR NOTE — one box per person, in your own words, autosaved as you write; it is never fed to the story writer, and it rides along when a story is shared, labeled as the family’s note. And In the margin — the structured corrections you pencil beside the record when a fact is wrong: they never change the record above, and they travel to the punch list, which carries them back to your source. A small ✎ beside a date or name means the margin disagrees with the record there.
