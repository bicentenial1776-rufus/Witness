The Screens

The margin & the punch list.

Witness treats your imported file as the record — it never edits your tree. But the moment you spot that a birth year is wrong is rarely the moment you’re sitting at Ancestry. So Witness gives you a margin to pencil the correction into, right beside the record, and a punch list that gathers everything still open — your corrections, the Tree Check’s findings, the orphan records — each row with its road back to the source.

How you get here: Tree tab → The punch list — or from the Research ledger, which counts the judgments you’ve already made; the punch list is the other half, what is still open. Corrections themselves are written on a person’s page, in the section below.

In the margin

At the bottom of every person’s page, past the register: In the margin. ✎ Correct the record › opens the add flow — pick the fact (Name, Birth, Death, Burial, the record’s own events, Parents, Spouse, or Other), read what the Record says, write what it Should be, and, if you want it on the worksheet, a line of why — the source or reasoning. The correction saves on an explicit button, and a small ✎ appears beside the fact it annotates, up in the vitals and on the lifeline.

1 2 3 4 5
  1. WHICH FACT? — the chips are the facts a correction can attach to: Name, Birth, Death, Burial, the record’s own events, Parents, Spouse, Other. The picked chip turns amber; picking a fact that already carries an open correction edits it rather than stacking a second.
  2. Should be… — your correction, in your words. When the record holds a value for the picked fact, a Record says line sits above the field so you write against what’s actually there (Spouse holds none here, so the field stands alone).
  3. The why — optional: the source or reasoning, if you want it riding along on the worksheet.
  4. Add to the margin — an explicit save, unlike the note box’s autosave: a correction is a structured row with a status, not a stream of prose. Once saved it appears as a card here, and a small ✎ rides beside the fact it annotates.
  5. The covenant — only you see these, and they never change the record above. They travel on the punch list; the fix itself belongs at the source.
The margin is not the note box. YOUR NOTE is prose in your own words, one running box per person. A correction is structured — a fact, a claimed value, a status — precisely so it can travel: onto the punch list, into the spreadsheet, and back to the source where the fix is actually made.

The punch list

One screen for everything still open, in three folds: Your corrections — to enter at the source, Tree Check findings, and Orphan records. The counts here are the same counts as the workbench screens — a finding marked fixed or ruled not an error there disappears here too, and a correction resolved here dims in the margin it came from. One decision, one truth, everywhere.

  1. ↓ Export this list as a spreadsheet — the whole list, all three folds, as one CSV worksheet: kind, category, person, reference number, the problem, your correction and note, and the link to that person at the source. Above it, the tally counts every open item together; “Nothing open” means your tree and your margin agree — until the next import finds something.
  2. A fold — tap ▾/▸ to open or close a section; each carries its own count. Corrections are yours; findings and orphans are the record’s. The counts here are the same counts as the workbench screens — one decision, one truth, everywhere.
  3. A correction card — the person, the fact, and Should be (with Record says above it when the record holds a value). Tapping the card opens that person’s page. If a fresh import has changed the fact under your pencil mark, an amber line says so — it may have been adopted — but Witness never resolves a correction for you.
  4. Entered at the source ✓ — press it once you’ve made the fix at the source; the correction retires here and dims in the margin it came from, where Reopen waits if you were wrong.
  5. Ancestry › — the road back: that person at the source your file came from (FamilySearch when the file did). The fix happens there; Witness holds the list.
  6. The other folds — Tree Check findings and orphan records, folded with their counts. Their rows are read-only here; the verdicts live on the workbenches.
The rhythm. Pencil the correction the moment you spot it, on the person’s page. When you next sit down at your source, open the punch list — or export the worksheet — and work down it. Mark each one Entered at the source ✓ as you go. Refresh your GEDCOM, and the record itself carries the fix. Your corrections survive that refresh: they move with the person, like your Tree Check marks, so nothing you’ve judged is ever asked twice.
On a wide screen the punch list is a split view: the list on the left, and each selected row’s person opens on the right, so you can judge and follow the source link without leaving the list.
