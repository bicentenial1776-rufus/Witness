The Screens

The margin & the punch list.

Witness treats your imported file as the record — it never edits your tree. But the moment you spot that a birth year is wrong is rarely the moment you’re sitting at Ancestry. So Witness gives you a margin to pencil the correction into, right beside the record, and a punch list that gathers everything still open — your corrections, the Tree Check’s findings, the orphan records — each row with its road back to the source.

How you get here: Tree tab → The punch list — or from the Research ledger, which counts the judgments you’ve already made; the punch list is the other half, what is still open. Corrections themselves are written on a person’s page, in the section below.

In the margin

At the bottom of every person’s page, past the register: In the margin. ✎ Correct the record › opens the add flow — pick the fact (Name, Birth, Death, Burial, the record’s own events, Parents, Spouse, or Other), read what the Record says, write what it Should be, and, if you want it on the worksheet, a line of why — the source or reasoning. The correction saves on an explicit button, and a small ✎ appears beside the fact it annotates, up in the vitals and on the lifeline.

Awaiting screenshot — shots/person-margin.png: the bottom of a person’s page with one open correction in the margin (Record says / Should be / an italic note) and the ✎ glyph visible beside the corrected fact above.
  1. The ✎ glyph — rides beside any fact that carries an open correction: the record still says what the record says, and the pencil mark tells you your margin disagrees.
  2. A correction card — the fact’s name in amber while open, Record says over Should be, your note in italics. Entered at the source ✓ retires it once you’ve made the fix at Ancestry; Erase removes the pencil mark entirely — the record itself was never touched either way.
  3. The covenant — the closing line says it plainly: only you see these, and they never change the record above. They travel on the punch list; the fix itself belongs at the source.
The margin is not the note box. YOUR NOTE is prose in your own words, one running box per person. A correction is structured — a fact, a claimed value, a status — precisely so it can travel: onto the punch list, into the spreadsheet, and back to the source where the fix is actually made.

The punch list

One screen for everything still open, in three folds: Your corrections — to enter at the source, Tree Check findings, and Orphan records. The counts here are the same counts as the workbench screens — a finding marked fixed or ruled not an error there disappears here too, and a correction resolved here dims in the margin it came from. One decision, one truth, everywhere.

Awaiting screenshot — shots/punch-list.png: the punch list with the open-items tally, the export link, and the corrections fold open on a card showing Record says / Should be, Entered at the source ✓, and the Ancestry link.
  1. The tally — every open item across all three folds, counted together. “Nothing open” means your tree and your margin agree — until the next import finds something.
  2. ↓ Export this list as a spreadsheet — the whole list as one CSV worksheet: kind, category, person, reference number, the problem, your correction and note, and the link to that person at the source. Built for a working session at Ancestry — the list travels, you go down it, and the tree comes back better.
  3. The three folds — tap ▸ to open a section; each carries its own count. Corrections are yours; findings and orphans are the record’s. Tapping any row opens that person’s page so you can judge the item against the full record.
  4. The road back — every row that can name its person at the source shows the link: Ancestry or FamilySearch, whichever your file came from. The fix happens there; Witness holds the list.
  5. “It may have been adopted” — an amber line on a correction whose fact the file has since changed: a fresh import altered what the record says under your pencil mark. Witness never resolves a correction for you — read the new value, and if the file now agrees with your margin, press Entered at the source ✓ yourself.
The rhythm. Pencil the correction the moment you spot it, on the person’s page. When you next sit down at your source, open the punch list — or export the worksheet — and work down it. Mark each one Entered at the source ✓ as you go. Refresh your GEDCOM, and the record itself carries the fix. Your corrections survive that refresh: they move with the person, like your Tree Check marks, so nothing you’ve judged is ever asked twice.
Findings and orphan rows are read-only here — the Mark fixed and Not an error verdicts live on the Tree Check and Orphan Records workbenches, where the full context sits. On a wide screen the punch list is a split view: the list on the left, and each selected row’s person opens on the right, so you can judge and follow the source link without leaving the list.
